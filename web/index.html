    What Moonlight can and cannot tell you

    It can show

    • medicines approved for a disease
    • medicines being tested for it
    • new connections worth reading about
    • questions to take to your doctor

    It cannot tell you

    • if a medicine will help or harm
    • which medicine is best
    • what is approved in your country
    • anything in place of your doctor
    For doctors: how far to trust each part
    • Source. Open Targets Platform.
    • “Approved”. In at least one country, per the database. Not a measure of efficacy.
    • Left out. Withdrawn anywhere, or approved nowhere: counted, never named. Unclear link to the disease: counted separately.
    • Wider and narrower diseases. Shown with a label, each in its own folded box.
    • Known gaps. A missing medicine may be withdrawn somewhere, filed further away, or not recorded yet.
    • Weak proof. Japan-list-only approvals say so. Approvals with no regulator record say so. Confirmed mistakes carry a red warning.
    • Ranking. Link strength across the top 25 proteins. One protein machine counts once.
    • AI. Gemini suggests which disease you mean, and Open Targets confirms it. Explanations are written by Gemini from the card’s records, or by Moonlight’s own template.

    Moonlight is not a prescribing reference.

    Where the facts come from
    • Facts. Open Targets Platform, a free and open research database.
    • Words. Explanations come from Google’s Gemini or Moonlight’s own template. What you type is sent to Gemini, so type no names or private details.
    • Visits. Counted with Umami. No cookies. Never the disease or anything you type.
